# Price-sheet player audit

The old production clues did not constrain the five full-bottle prices. Even after matching the half-bottle, 120 assignments remained. Barrel and lees facts did not determine the exact purchase prices.

The revised sheet gives receipt-specific comparisons linked to sensory styles and the anonymous tasting notes. These are game clues, not general claims that oak determines price.

| Information used | Remaining assignments |
| --- | ---: |
| Six price tags | 720 |
| Sweet wine is the half-bottle | 120 |
| Dry whites are the cheapest and most expensive | 12 |
| Reds are below 250 and above 400 | 4 |
| Oak wording identifies the pricier red and white within their pairs | 1 |

Checked all 720 permutations against the actual Imeto Julebord data. The sole result matches the receipt. This does not guarantee people identify aromas correctly: that is the tasting part of the puzzle.

The option numbers, bottle order and host answer key are unchanged.
