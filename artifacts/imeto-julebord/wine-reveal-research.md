# Wine reveal research

Checked 13 November 2026. Editorial copy is in `lib/wine-reveals.ts`, keyed by Polet number, so both the real tasting and test tasting use it without changing saved wine facts or round state.

Each reveal has a short producer story, two highlights and a tasting prompt. Flavour suggestions invite comparison; they do not claim that every taster will perceive a particular aroma. Longer Norwegian Polet notes and research links remain expandable.

## A · Frédéric Thiébault Brut Premier Cru · 21132301

- [Producer’s Brut page](https://www.champagnethiebaultfrederic.fr/store/en/champagnes/champagne-brut/): three years in the cellar; 40% Meunier, 35% Pinot Noir, 25% Chardonnay. The general page currently says **30% reserve wine and 9.8 g/L dosage**.
- [Champagne growers’ producer profile](https://www.champagnedevignerons.fr/vignerons/champagne-frederic-thiebault/): Frédéric is the fifth generation, Fabian is his son, address Pargny-lès-Reims.
- [Comité Champagne: maturation](https://www.champagne.fr/en/about-champagne/how-champagne-is-made/maturation): general background on lees maturation. Bread/pastry is a possible result, not proof of a particular technique from aroma alone.
- The bottle-specific Polet record, saved in `lib/receipt-wine-facts.json`, says **40% reserve wine, 36 months on lees, 7.3 g/L sugar**. Reveal explicitly attributes 40% to Polet. Dosage is an addition at disgorgement and is not interchangeable with the final analytical sugar figure. No attempt to reconcile different releases by inventing a cause. Producer site includes a country/age notice, so the growers’ profile is also provided.

## B · Famille Paquet Mâcon-Lugny Les Charmes 2025 · 10644301

- [Producer’s cuvée page](https://famille-paquet.com/macon-lugny-les-charmes/?lang=en): Chardonnay, six to seven months entirely in vats, jasmine, orchard fruit, citrus and white flowers.
- [Family history](https://famille-paquet.com/our-history/?lang=en) and [French family/approach page](https://famille-paquet.com/la-famille/): Jocelyne and Michel founded Domaine des Valanges in 1980; three sons Jean-Baptiste, Mathieu and Camille; Camille manages vines and cellar.
- Polet’s 2025 record: seven months on lees, natural yeast, gentle filtration; apple/citrus/herbs/nuts in its tasting note. Lees can contribute fullness; tank ageing is a cuvée-level producer description, not a separately verified 2025 cellar log.
- [2020 technical sheet](https://famille-paquet.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/FT-FR-2020-MACONLUGNY.pdf) also confirms the tank approach, but its harvest conditions, parcel count and precise fermentation details are **not presented as 2025 facts**.

## C · Rémi Jobard Bourgogne Blanc 2024 · 20576901

- [DVP’s producer profile](https://www.dvp-vins-domaines.com/en/domain/91/domaine-remi-jobard): succession from Charles in 1993, removal of herbicides, organic estate farming, Meursault base. DVP distributes the wines; this is labelled a distributor profile, **not Jobard’s own website**. No standalone producer website was verified.
- [DVP-hosted Bourgogne Chardonnay sheet](https://vincod.com/J0I21E/get/tech-sheet/3731PY2VNB): the producer’s general aim is precision without conspicuous woody aromas. Used only for that producer-level intention, not to assert this bottle’s vineyard source or exact ageing.
- Polet’s exact product lists Chardonnay, citrus/apple, some oak and a fresh mineral finish. The comparison with Paquet is a tasting exercise, not a prediction that one is richer or better. NOK 449.90 is over twice NOK 209.90.
- Other listings include Bourgogne Côte d’Or Vieilles Vignes, Bourgogne Chardonnay and purchased-fruit bottlings. Vineyard origin, vine age, Stockinger barrels, ageing duration, certification date and an exact négoce classification were **not reliably matched to 20576901**, so none is asserted. A Meursault-based producer does not make this a Meursault-appellation wine.

## D · La Pascerette des Vignes Cuvée Saint Vincent Pinot Noir 2025 · 16786001

- [Producer’s history](https://www.pascerettedesvignes.com/): Céline Robergeot-Cienki is a trained engineer, founded the domaine in January 2013; husband Vincent joined her in 2019; sheep and vineyards are both part of the farm; name explained through “pascer”, to graze.
- Polet’s exact Saint Vincent product supplies strawberry/cherry/herbs/anise, fairly soft texture and **under 3 g/L sugar**. Soft tannin and ripe fruit do not establish sweetness. The suggestion to notice grip is a sensory prompt.
- The [producer’s general winemaking page](https://www.pascerettedesvignes.com/wines) mentions manual harvest and malolactic fermentation, but was not enough to verify every detail for this cuvée.
- Importer descriptions of a different Bourgogne Rouge/Creux d’Arban include old vines and 16 months in used barrels. **Those details are not transferred to Saint Vincent.** Polet lists only France as origin; no more precise vineyard origin is added. “Tradisjonell metode” in this still red’s raw method field is not interpreted as sparkling-wine production.

## E · Domaine Belleville Rully Chaponnière 2023 · 20063201

- [Producer’s exact cuvée page](https://www.domainebelleville.com/en/wines/11/rully-quot-chaponniere-quot): “chapons” means cuttings; named east-facing clay-limestone site with clay predominating; roughly 40-year-old vines; Pinot Noir; 12 months ageing, 15% new barrels; red fruit with a touch of smoke and savoury character.
- Polet’s 2023 record independently confirms vines planted in the 1980s, east-facing parcel, **12 months in French oak, 15% new**. Its tasting note adds forest floor/herbs/anise.
- Mostly used oak can give a less overt wood impression than new barrels. The reveal invites a search for subtle smoke/savouriness, without claiming that every smoky or forest-floor aroma comes from oak. No claim that soil minerals directly taste of limestone.

## F · Madame de Rayne Sauternes 2021 · 19899302

- [Producer’s Madame de Rayne page](https://www.raynevigneau.fr/fr/3-madame-de-rayne) and [English cuvée sheet](https://www.raynevigneau.fr/document/vins/Madame-de-Rayne-EN.pdf): named after Catherine de Pontac/Madame de Rayne, bought estate in 1834, first-growth classification in 1855; second wine intended to emphasise youth/freshness/finesse. Classification belongs to the estate, not a separate award to the second wine.
- Polet’s exact 2021 half-bottle record: hand selection of botrytised grapes; 105 g/L sugar; 6.3 g/L acid; 18 months in oak, 30% new; honey/apricot/citrus/dried fruit. The reveal uses the first three details for the tasting explanation, leaving room to keep it short.
- [UC Davis: Botrytis cinerea](https://wine.ucdavis.edu/industry-info/enology/wine-microbiology/yeast-mold/botrytis-cinerea): noble rot can cause water loss and concentration. This is the mechanism behind the explanation, not a claim that the fungus was deliberately inoculated.
- The general estate sheet’s 74% Sémillon/24% Sauvignon/2% Muscadelle describes **vineyard planting**, not a verified 2021 bottle blend. Polet lists 100% Sémillon for this product. No blend is newly asserted in the story. No claims from other Madame de Rayne vintages are transferred to 2021.
